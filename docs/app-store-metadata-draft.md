## Description

Point, shoot, and let the photograph develop.

Aperture brings a small collection of film-inspired looks to your iPhone camera. Choose 1998, Night or Cinema, take a photograph, and find the developed result in your private Lab. Warm colour, grain, soft highlights and occasional light leaks give each look its own character—without an editing workflow getting in the way.

- Capture photographs or short films with sound, up to 60 seconds.
- Use front and rear cameras, tap to focus, and choose the lens stops your iPhone supports.
- Keep your favourites together in the Lab, then share or save developed media to Photos.
- Keep an untouched original if you want one, or let Aperture keep only the developed result.
- Choose light leaks and photo quality. The 1998 look includes a compact current-date stamp; Night and Cinema use your date-stamp settings.
- Develop on your iPhone, without an account, advertising or analytics.

Your Lab is separate from Photos. Aperture asks only for add access when you export or enable auto-save; it does not need to read your Photos library. Copies you share, export or back up through system services are managed separately.

Made for iPhone. Camera features depend on your device. Camera permission is required to shoot; microphone permission is required to record films with sound.

## App Review notes draft

Aperture is an account-free, local film camera for iPhone. No login/demo account or backend is required.

- Camera permission is requested to show the live viewfinder and capture. Denying it leaves the Lab and Settings accessible, with an explanation and Settings recovery action.
- Choosing Video requests microphone permission because films include synchronized sound. Recordings stop at 60 seconds.
- Capture is saved locally before development. Finished items appear in the Lab, where reviewers can open, favourite, share, export or delete them.
- Photos permission is add-only and requested when exporting or auto-saving; Photos read access is not requested.
- A physical iPhone is needed to review camera capture, lenses, autofocus, flash and synchronized microphone recording. Simulator screenshots/renderer tests are not evidence of those hardware functions.
- The fitted 1998 look includes its date stamp; Night and Cinema use the configurable date-stamp settings.
