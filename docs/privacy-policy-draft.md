# Aperture privacy policy — publication draft

**Owner review required before publication.** Supply the publisher's legal identity, a real support/privacy contact and an effective date. Publish on the approved host, verify the public URL, and link it in App Store Connect and Settings. This repository file does not complete those launch gates.

## Your photographs stay under your control

Aperture is an iPhone camera that captures photographs and short films and develops their film look on your iPhone. It does not require an account. The current app has no advertising, analytics SDKs, tracking, app-operated upload service or third-party runtime dependencies. Aperture does not collect or transmit your photographs, recordings or usage data to a server operated by the publisher.

## Permissions

- **Camera:** used when you choose to take photographs or record films.
- **Microphone:** used for synchronized sound when you choose Video. Recording with sound requires microphone permission.
- **Photos, add only:** used when you export developed media or enable auto-save. Aperture does not request access to read your Photos library.

You can manage permissions in iPhone Settings. The local Lab remains available without camera access, and your local media remains safe if Photos export is denied or fails.

## Local storage and processing

Captured and developed media, capture metadata, the applied film recipe, preferences and rebuildable thumbnails are stored in Aperture's app storage on your iPhone. Keeping a separate untouched original is optional. Processing and recovery use the saved recipe so an existing capture does not change when you change current settings.

## Exporting, sharing and system services

Exporting creates a copy in Apple Photos. Sharing uses the iOS share sheet and sends the selected media to the destination you choose; the destination's own privacy practices apply. Photos synchronization, iPhone/app backups, device diagnostics and other Apple services are controlled by your system settings and Apple's policies. These are separate from Aperture's local processing; the app cannot promise that a user-authorized share or system-managed backup never leaves the device.

## Deletion and retention

Deleting an item in the Lab removes Aperture's current local media files and associated rebuildable thumbnail cache. Copies you previously exported, shared or backed up must be managed separately in those destinations. Legacy installations may contain older source files preserved for safe migration/recovery; the app keeps a local deletion ledger so deleted imports do not reappear. Deleting the app removes its local app container, subject to iOS backup/restore behavior. Export anything you want to keep before deleting the app.
